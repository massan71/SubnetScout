## What's new: known vulnerabilities (CVE)

- **CVE lookup from service versions.** nmap recognizes the product and version of services (e.g. `openssh 9.6p1`) and SubnetScout looks up the matching CVEs in **NIST's NVD** database.
- **Privacy.** It is **off by default**: turn it on with "Look up known vulnerabilities (CVE)" in the sidebar or in Settings (⌘,). **Only the software name and version** are sent to NVD, never addresses, names or other device data.
- **One finding per vulnerable service.** It has the severity of the worst CVE and shows the number of CVEs, the highest CVSS and how many are **actively exploited** according to the CISA KEV catalog. CVEs in the KEV count as at least "High".
- **Device details.** There is a list of CVEs per port, with score, description, date and a link to the NVD page. The HTML report also has a new "Known vulnerabilities" section.
- **Cache and speed.** Responses are cached on the Mac for 7 days. A free **NVD API key**, stored in the Keychain, makes the first lookup about 10 times faster.

Matching is based on the version the services report. Routers, NAS and cameras often fix flaws without changing the version: verify the results on the device.
